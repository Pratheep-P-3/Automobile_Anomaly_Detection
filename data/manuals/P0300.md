# P0300 - Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire Detected

## System
Powertrain (Ignition / Fuel Delivery)

## Description
The Powertrain Control Module (PCM) has detected misfire events across multiple cylinders,
not isolated to a single cylinder. This is determined by monitoring crankshaft speed
fluctuations over a rolling window of engine revolutions. Random misfires can cause
catalytic converter damage if left unresolved.

## Possible Causes
- Worn or fouled spark plugs
- Failing ignition coil(s) or coil pack
- Vacuum leak causing lean air/fuel mixture
- Clogged or malfunctioning fuel injectors
- Low fuel pressure from a failing fuel pump
- Damaged or leaking head gasket
- Contaminated or low-quality fuel

## Diagnostic Steps
1. Retrieve freeze frame data to identify conditions when the misfire occurred.
2. Inspect spark plugs and ignition coils for wear, carbon tracking, or damage.
3. Check for vacuum leaks using a smoke test or listening for hissing near intake manifold.
4. Measure fuel rail pressure against manufacturer specification.
5. Perform a cylinder balance/power contribution test to confirm multiple cylinders are affected.
6. Inspect for exhaust or intake manifold leaks that could affect multiple cylinders.

## Recommended Service Actions
- Replace spark plugs and ignition coils if worn or out of specification.
- Repair vacuum leaks identified during smoke testing.
- Clean or replace clogged fuel injectors.
- Replace fuel pump if fuel pressure is below specification.
- Do not continue driving for extended periods with an active multi-cylinder misfire;
  it can cause catalytic converter overheating and permanent damage.

## Parts / Tools
Spark plug socket set, ignition coil tester, fuel pressure gauge, smoke machine, OBD-II scan tool.

## Safety Notes
A flashing check engine light combined with a misfire code indicates a severe misfire.
Advise the customer to reduce engine load and schedule immediate service to prevent
catalytic converter damage.
