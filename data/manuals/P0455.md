# P0455 - EVAP System Large Leak Detected

## System
Powertrain (Evaporative Emissions)

## Description
The Evaporative Emission Control (EVAP) system monitor has detected a large leak, most
commonly caused by an improperly sealed fuel cap or a large opening somewhere in the
EVAP system.

## Possible Causes
- Loose, missing, or damaged fuel cap
- Cracked EVAP hoses or canister
- Faulty purge valve or vent valve stuck open
- Damaged fuel tank or filler neck

## Diagnostic Steps
1. Check the fuel cap first for proper sealing, cracks, or missing O-ring.
2. Perform an EVAP smoke test to visually locate the leak source.
3. Inspect purge and vent valve operation using bidirectional scan tool controls.
4. Inspect fuel tank and filler neck for physical damage.

## Recommended Service Actions
- Replace the fuel cap if it is damaged or not sealing correctly (most common fix).
- Repair or replace cracked hoses or the EVAP canister as identified by the smoke test.
- Replace the purge or vent valve if it fails a bidirectional test.

## Parts / Tools
EVAP smoke machine, OBD-II scan tool with bidirectional controls, replacement fuel cap.

## Safety Notes
Low severity from a drivability standpoint. Recommend checking the fuel cap immediately
(free, quick fix) before scheduling a full EVAP system diagnostic.
