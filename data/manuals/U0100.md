# U0100 - Lost Communication with ECM/PCM

## System
Network (Vehicle Communication Bus / CAN)

## Description
Another control module on the vehicle network has reported a loss of communication with
the Engine Control Module / Powertrain Control Module (ECM/PCM). This typically points to
a power, ground, or CAN bus wiring issue rather than the ECM/PCM itself.

## Possible Causes
- Blown fuse or loss of power/ground to the ECM/PCM
- Damaged, pinched, or corroded CAN bus wiring
- Failing or unresponsive ECM/PCM
- Aftermarket accessory interfering with the CAN bus
- Corroded battery terminals causing intermittent power loss

## Diagnostic Steps
1. Check ECM/PCM power and ground circuits, including relevant fuses and relays.
2. Inspect battery terminals and main grounds for corrosion or looseness.
3. Test CAN bus resistance (should read approximately 60 ohms across the bus with
   key off) to check for wiring damage.
4. Check for aftermarket wiring/accessories tapped into the vehicle harness.
5. If wiring and power are confirmed good, test or substitute the ECM/PCM.

## Recommended Service Actions
- Repair power/ground faults or blown fuses first.
- Repair damaged CAN bus wiring identified during resistance testing.
- Remove or correct any interfering aftermarket wiring.
- Replace the ECM/PCM only after wiring and power have been fully verified as good.

## Parts / Tools
Digital multimeter, CAN bus resistance test, manufacturer diagnostic software, fuse/relay kit.

## Safety Notes
Intermittent loss of ECM/PCM communication can cause the vehicle to stall unexpectedly
or fail to start, which is a safety risk in traffic. Recommend the vehicle not be driven
until repaired; advise towing to the service center if symptoms are active.
